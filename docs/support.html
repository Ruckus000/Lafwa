Frequently Asked Questions

Does Lafwa work without internet?

Yes! After you download the app, everything works 100% offline. You can read the Bible, search hymns, and use all features without any internet connection.

How do I switch between Kreyòl and French?

In the Bible reader, tap the language toggle in the header (shows "KR" or "FR"). Your choice is remembered for future sessions. You can also change the default language in Settings.

How do I use Presentation Mode for church?

Open any hymn and tap the presentation icon (expand/fullscreen icon) in the header. This displays lyrics in large text on a black background, perfect for projecting. Tap or swipe to move between verses. Swipe down to exit.

Can I jump directly to a hymn number?

Yes! On the Hymns tab, tap the search icon and type the hymn number. You can also use the numeric keypad quick-jump feature to go directly to any hymn.

How do I bookmark a verse?

While reading the Bible, long-press on any verse to select it. Then tap the bookmark icon in the action menu that appears. Access your bookmarks from the More tab.

Where is my data stored?

All your bookmarks, highlights, favorites, and reading history are stored locally on your device only. We don't have access to your data, and it doesn't sync to any cloud. See our Privacy Policy for details.

How do I change the font size?

Go to the More tab → Settings → Font Size. You can choose from XS, S, M, L, or XL. The change applies to both Bible and hymn reading.

Is there a dark mode?

Yes! Go to More → Settings → Theme. You can choose Light, Dark, or System (follows your device setting). Dark mode is easier on the eyes for nighttime reading.

Will my data be lost if I uninstall the app?

Yes, since all data is stored locally on your device. If you uninstall Lafwa or clear app data, your bookmarks, highlights, and favorites will be permanently deleted. We recommend keeping the app installed.

How many hymns are included?

Lafwa includes all 336 hymns from Chant d'Espérance, the beloved Haitian hymnal used in Protestant churches throughout Haiti and the diaspora.
